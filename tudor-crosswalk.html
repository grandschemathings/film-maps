Two Tellings of the Tudors

Both playlists are already in historical order, so laying them side by side turns them into a crosswalk. On the left, three BBC2 studio serials and a 1971 feature, made between 1970 and 1972. On the right, three series and a film adapted from Philippa Gregory between 2008 and 2020. The question is where the two tell the same year — and the answer is a narrow band with long tails at both ends.

How the links were made

Every episode title in both playlists carries a bracketed year‑span, worked out from the episode descriptions rather than from any year spoken on screen — these shows almost never say a date aloud. A link is drawn wherever two spans overlap. That is arithmetic, not judgement, which is why there are 42 of them.

An event name is editorial; a year is derived. Thirteen links carry the name of something that actually happens inside the overlap window. The build refuses to attach an event whose date falls outside the overlap — which caught one on the first run: Henry VII’s marriage to Elizabeth of York is January 1486, so it cannot hang on Crown in Jeopardy, bracketed 1485. It moved to Power in the Land.

The two features carry no bracket, so their spans are written by hand — Mary, Queen of Scots 1561–87 and The Other Boleyn Girl 1521–36 — and are marked set in the diagram rather than passed off as derived.

Height is running order, not a time axis. Each column is simply its playlist, top to bottom, so the vertical gap between two boxes is a count of episodes and not a number of years. Aligning the columns on one shared date was tried and rejected: the two tell their years at such different speeds that matching them at Bosworth only moves the distortion to the 1520s. Nothing may be read straight across the page — a link is the only claim of a shared year, and the centre channel is the only place time runs evenly.

What the shape shows

One episode carries seventeen. The Six Wives of Henry VIII gives Catherine of Aragon a single 90‑minute play bracketed 1501–1536; Gregory spends seventeen episodes on the same years. The fan spreading from that one box is the whole difference between the two projects — one compresses a life into an evening, the other dilates it across two series.

The tails never meet. Gregory’s first nine parts run 1464–1483, before the BBC trilogy opens; the BBC’s last ten run 1539–1603, after Gregory stops. Bosworth is the hinge: The White Queen ends there and The Shadow of the Tower begins there, which is why the top‑left link is the only place the two collections touch end to end.

There is a hole in the middle of the BBC trilogy. Traitors (1491–92) is the one Gregory episode inside the shared window with no counterpart at all: the BBC jumps from 1487 straight to 1494, so Perkin Warbeck’s first appearance in Cork happens off‑screen on the left.

# Film Maps

Views onto a history film collection. The maps place each film by **where it is set** — not
where it was shot and not where the studio was. The syllabus asks a different question:
what could you actually teach from this shelf?

- **[Africa on Film](https://grandschemathings.github.io/film-maps/africa.html)** —
  170 films, 157 placements, 32 countries. Grey means no films yet; films tagged Africa but
  deliberately excluded are listed, so an omission is a decision on record.
- **[South American Cinema](https://grandschemathings.github.io/film-maps/south-america.html)** —
  every country from the 1530s conquest to the present, filterable by theme, and splittable
  between films made by South Americans and films made by outsiders.

- **[History of Russia — a film syllabus](https://grandschemathings.github.io/film-maps/russia-course.html)** —
  thirty sessions across three terms, Kievan Rus to Navalny, in which the weekly work is a
  film rather than a book. 82 films placed from a 93-film shelf; titles the syllabus wants
  and the shelf lacks are marked *not held*, so a thin week reads as a gap in the collection
  rather than in the history. Modelled on the
  [Institute for the Study of Western Civilization](https://westernciv.com/syllabus/history-of-russia/)
  syllabus, which pairs one spine text with a different supplementary work each week.

- **[History of England — a film syllabus](https://grandschemathings.github.io/film-maps/england-course.html)** —
  Roman Britain to the miners' strike, 139 films placed from a 146-item shelf. The deepest
  shelf here for television, so several weeks rest on a serial rather than a feature.
- **[History of France — a film syllabus](https://grandschemathings.github.io/film-maps/france-course.html)** —
  Vercingetorix to the banlieue. The deepest collection here, and the clearest case that a national
  course is inherently cross-shelf: fifty of the films it needs live under Ancient Rome, the World
  Wars, the Holocaust shelf and Africa.
- **[History of Spain — a film syllabus](https://grandschemathings.github.io/film-maps/spain-course.html)** —
  Al-Andalus to the present in twenty-four sessions rather than thirty, because the shelf is thinner
  and padding it would misrepresent it. The Reconquista and Golden Age rest almost entirely on
  series, and every Conquest film sits on the MesoAmerica shelf.
- **[English Literature on Film — an extension](https://grandschemathings.github.io/film-maps/england-literature-extension.html)** —
  Shakespeare to Tolkien, following the books rather than the history. 585 films in the collection
  are adaptations and 570 are not on the Literature shelf; Austen alone sits across seven shelves.
  A shelf needs a physical move, which is why only two authors have one. A syllabus does not.
- **[Russian Cinema — an extension](https://grandschemathings.github.io/film-maps/russia-film-extension.html)** —
  the optional side trip to the Russia course, on the model of an alumni travel brochure.
  The main course treats these films as evidence about the past; the extension treats them as
  events. Films recurring between the two are the point: *Andrei Rublev* is the fifteenth
  century there and Tarkovsky here.

The two maps load D3 and their country geometry from a CDN, so they need a network
connection. The syllabus is self-contained.

These are published copies. The catalogue they are built from is private; the maps are
regenerated there and copied here, so this repo is a destination rather than a source.
